# Captain Sonar — Real-Time Teaching Script (8 Players)

Oct 2, 2026 · @Seth

## Notes for the teacher (don't read aloud)

This script takes about 20–25 minutes to read, then a 5-minute practice run. Read the plain text aloud; lines in *italics in brackets* are stage directions for you.

- **Assumed setup:** Alpha map, Real-Time (dark) side of every sheet, Hunt mode (4 damage to win), 4 players per team in this order across the screen: Radio Operator, Captain, First Mate, Engineer.
- **Teach by station, not by rulebook order.** Everyone hears every role, but each person only has to master one. Tell people to keep their eyes on their own sheet when you reach their role.
- **Point at sheets as you go.** Each role section ends with a one-line "your job in one sentence" — have that player repeat it back.
- **Slow down on the hard parts.** Silence, Mines and Surfacing are the three things new crews get wrong. Give them extra time; all three get a dry run in the practice round.
- **Decide two rulings before you start.** The rulebook doesn't say whether a Mine can be dropped on a diagonal dot, or whether a torpedo's path can pass through islands. Pick an answer for each and announce it, so nobody argues mid-game.
- **Have both Engineers sit where they can see each other's sheet** for the surfacing check.

## 1. The hook

Welcome aboard. Tonight we're two submarine crews, one on each side of this screen, hunting each other through the same patch of ocean.

Here's the twist: there are no turns. Both submarines move at the same time, as fast as your crew can handle. Your Captain shouts out every move, the enemy hears every word, and they're trying to work out where you are from those words alone.

So the whole game is a race between two things. Can you find them before they find you? And can your crew keep the boat running while you go as fast as you dare? Speed wins games, but a sloppy crew sinks its own submarine.

The good news: nobody has to learn everything. Each of you has exactly one job. I'll explain all four jobs so you understand what your teammates are doing, but you only need to master yours.

## 2. How you win

We're playing Hunt mode. Each submarine can take four damage. Deal the fourth damage to the enemy and you win.

*\[Point to the four damage boxes at the top right of the First Mate's sheet.\]*

There are three ways a submarine takes damage:

1. **Getting hit by enemy weapons** — torpedoes and mines. A direct hit is 2 damage. A near miss, one dot away including diagonals, is 1 damage.
2. **Hitting yourself** — if your own torpedo or mine goes off right next to you, you take damage too.
3. **Breaking your own submarine** — if the Engineer's sheet fills up the wrong way, the boat takes 1 damage. I'll explain that in the Engineer section.

So you can lose by being found, by being reckless, or by being disorganized. Remember that last one; it happens to new crews a lot.

*\[Optional, if anyone asks: the rulebook also has a Sudden Death mode where the first hit of any kind wins. It's fast and brutal; we're using Hunt so the game has time to breathe.\]*

## 3. The four stations

*\[Point to each seat as you name it, on both sides of the screen.\]*

On each team, from one end of the table to the other:

| Seat | Role | One-line job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Radio Operator** | Listens to the *enemy* Captain and tracks where their sub is. |
| 2 | **Captain** | Steers our sub, fires weapons, makes the calls. |
| 3 | **First Mate** | Charges up our weapons and gadgets, tracks our damage. |
| 4 | **Engineer** | Tracks breakdowns caused by moving, keeps the sub from falling apart. |

Notice the Captain sits in the middle. The Captain is the hub: the Radio Operator feeds them information about the enemy, and the First Mate and Engineer tell them what the sub can and can't do right now.

The screen stays up the whole game. Never look at the other team's sheets. The only exception is one moment during surfacing, which I'll explain later.

## 4. The heartbeat of the game

If you remember one thing, remember this. Every time a Captain moves the submarine one dot, four things happen:

1. **Our Captain** says loudly: "Heading North" — or East, South, or West — and draws the line on their map.
2. **Our First Mate** marks one box on any system gauge — a charge meter for a weapon or gadget — then says "OK."
3. **Our Engineer** marks one breakdown — a bit of wear and tear — in the matching direction, then says "OK."
4. **The enemy Radio Operator** hears "North" and draws it on their tracking sheet.

The Captain may not call the next direction until they've heard **both** OKs. That's the chain of command, and it's the speed limit of your submarine.

So: Captain moves, First Mate charges, Engineer breaks, enemy listens. Repeat as fast as you can manage.

*\[Have everyone say it together once: "Move, charge, break, listen."\]*

## 5. The Captain

*\[Captains, look at your map.\]*

The map is a 15-by-15 grid: letters A to O across, numbers 1 to 15 down. It's split into nine big numbered sectors. The blobs are islands. Subs move along the water dots.

**Before the game:** secretly draw an X on any water dot. That's where you start. Not on an island. Don't let the other team see.

**Starting:** when both Captains are ready, you both shout "Dive!" together, and the game is live.

**Moving:** you move one dot at a time, North, South, East, or West — never diagonally. Say it loud and clear: "Heading North." Then draw the line on your map. Wait for your First Mate's and Engineer's OK before the next move.

**Three route rules:**

- You can never cross an island.
- You can never cross or touch your own line. Once you've been on a dot, it's off limits.
- You can't leave the edge of the map.

That second rule is the trap. Your line grows longer and longer, and if you box yourself in with nowhere to go, that's a **Blackout**: you must surface immediately. The only way to erase your line is to surface, which I'll cover later.

**Your other orders:** besides moving, the Captain is the one who fires Torpedoes, drops and detonates Mines, uses Silence, and decides to Surface. The First Mate handles the two scanning systems, Drone and Sonar.

**Captain, your job in one sentence:** steer us safely, listen to your crew, and pull the trigger at the right moment.

## 6. The First Mate

*\[First Mates, look at your sheet.\]*

You have six round gauges, in three colors:

- **Red — weapons:** Mine and Torpedo.
- **Green — detection:** Drone and Sonar.
- **Yellow — special:** Silence, and Scenario. We're not using Scenario on this map, so ignore it.

Every time your Captain announces a move, you mark **one** empty box on **any** gauge you like, then say "OK." Your Captain may tell you what to charge — follow their lead — but if they don't, use your judgment.

When a gauge is full, announce it out loud: "Torpedo ready!" Now the Captain knows they can fire. Count the boxes: some gauges fill fast, Silence takes a while.

A full gauge stays full until it's used. When the system is used, you erase that whole gauge and start charging it again.

**You personally fire two systems: Drone and Sonar.** The Captain fires everything else. I'll explain what they do shortly.

**You also keep score.** Those four damage boxes at the top right are our health. Every time we take damage, you cross one off. Four crossed off and we lose.

**First Mate, your job in one sentence:** charge what the Captain needs, shout when it's ready, and track our damage.

## 7. The Engineer

*\[Engineers, look at your sheet. Everyone else, this one affects you too.\]*

Moving wears out the submarine. Your sheet has four panels: West, North, South, East. Every time the Captain moves, you cross out **one** symbol of your choice in the panel matching that direction, then say "OK." Captain goes North, you cross something in the North panel.

**What the symbols mean:**

- **Red, green, yellow symbols** match the First Mate's system colors. If even one red symbol is crossed out anywhere, Mine and Torpedo are broken. One green: Drone and Sonar are broken. One yellow: Silence is broken.
- **Radiation symbols** — the trefoil hazard icons — do nothing on their own, but they're dangerous in bulk.

Broken systems can still be *charged* by the First Mate. They just can't be *used* until repaired. So before anyone fires a system, they ask you: "Any red breakdowns?" If someone orders a broken system anyway, it doesn't work — and you must cross out a radiation symbol as a penalty.

**The two ways we hurt ourselves:**

1. **A full panel.** If every symbol in one direction's panel is crossed out, we take 1 damage.
2. **All radiation.** If every radiation symbol on the sheet is crossed out, we take 1 damage.

Either way, shout "Damage!", the First Mate crosses off a health box, the Captain announces it to the enemy, and then you erase your **entire** sheet. A fresh start, but it cost us.

**The free repair: circuits.** *\[Point to the colored lines along the top half of the panels.\]* The top half of each panel is the Central Circuits. Each line links four symbols, usually spread over two or more panels — so finishing one often means asking the Captain for a specific direction. Cross out all four symbols on one line and they repair themselves — erase just those four. If a single mark would both complete a circuit and cause damage, the repair wins and no damage happens.

The bottom half is the Reactor. Nothing there repairs itself; only surfacing or taking damage clears it.

So your real job is steering the Captain. Speak up: "Can we go East? It finishes a circuit." Or: "Don't go West again, that panel is almost full."

**Engineer, your job in one sentence:** mark the breakdowns, complete circuits, and warn the Captain before we break.

## 8. The Radio Operator

*\[Radio Operators, pick up your clear plastic sheet.\]*

You're the only person on the team who doesn't care what our Captain says. You listen to the **enemy** Captain.

You have a copy of the map and a clear overlay. Start by drawing a dot in the **middle** of the clear sheet — not on any particular map spot, because you don't know where they started. Every time the enemy Captain says a direction, draw one step in that direction on the clear sheet.

Now you have the *shape* of their route, but not its position. Slide the overlay around on your map. Their route can't cross islands and can't cross itself, so most positions are impossible. When they surface, their old route is wiped, so it no longer blocks them. The places where the shape fits are where they might be. The longer their route, the fewer places it fits.

Also write down every other clue the enemy gives away. I'll explain each of these systems in a minute:

- **Silence** — they moved up to four dots in a straight line, direction unknown. Your track gets fuzzier.
- **Torpedo impact** — they're within four dots of where it landed.
- **Mine detonation** — their route passed right next to that dot when they dropped it.
- **Surfacing** — they announce which sector they're in.
- **Damage from their Engineer** — no position info, but it tells you they're struggling.
- **Drone and Sonar answers** from our own scans.

If your drawing runs off the edge of the clear sheet or you lose track, erase it and restart from the latest clues. That's normal.

And talk constantly. Tell the Captain things like: "They're in sector 4 or 7, probably near E8." A great Radio Operator wins games — the rulebook calls them "Golden Ears."

**Radio Operator, your job in one sentence:** draw every enemy move, figure out where they are, and keep the Captain informed.

## 9. Using systems: "Stop!"

This is the one moment the real-time chaos pauses.

**How to activate any system:**

1. The gauge must be full, and the Engineer must confirm no breakdown of that color.
2. Raise your fist and shout **"Stop!"** Everyone at the table — both teams — lifts their pen and listens.
3. Say what you're doing: "Torpedo!" or "Drone!"
4. Resolve it. The First Mate erases that gauge.
5. Play resumes.

**One limit:** you can't use two systems back to back. Your Captain must make at least one move between activations.

Now the five systems.

### Torpedo — Captain, red

Pick a target dot up to **four steps** away from you, counting North/South/East/West steps, not diagonals. Announce it: "Torpedo, impact at C6!" You don't draw the path.

The enemy Captain answers honestly:

- **"Clear"** — it landed two or more dots away from them.
- **"Indirect hit"** — it landed on one of the eight dots around them, diagonals included. 1 damage.
- **"Direct hit"** — it landed right on them. 2 damage.

Careful: if it lands right next to *you*, you take damage too. And firing gives away information — the enemy now knows you're within four dots of that impact.

### Mine — Captain, red

Dropping and detonating are separate.

**Drop:** Stop, then draw an M on a dot next to your sub, not on your own route. Say "Mine dropped!" — but not where. It does nothing yet.

**Detonate:** later, any time, raise your fist and shout "Stop, I'm blowing up the mine at G7!" It damages exactly like a torpedo: direct 2, adjacent 1, and it can hurt you too if you're next to it. Detonating doesn't need a full gauge and doesn't count as a system use, but you can't detonate with a red breakdown.

Mines stay on your map when you surface. A torpedo landing on a mine destroys it; a torpedo landing next to a mine sets it off.

### Drone — First Mate, green

Ask the enemy about one sector: "Are you in sector 5?" They must answer yes or no truthfully.

### Sonar — First Mate, green

The enemy Captain must tell you two facts about their position, picking from row, column, or sector — two **different** kinds. **One is true, one is false**, and they don't say which. For example, at N12 in sector 9 they might say "Column N and sector 7."

### Silence — Captain, yellow

Your sneaky move. Shout "Stop! Silence!" then move zero to four dots in a straight line **without saying the direction**. Show your Engineer the direction with your thumb behind the screen.

It still counts as a move: the First Mate marks one gauge — it can even be the Silence gauge they just emptied — and the Engineer marks one breakdown in that direction. If the Captain moves zero, the Engineer pretends to mark something. Island and route rules still apply.

Enemy Radio Operators: this is where your tracking gets hard.

## 10. Surfacing

Surfacing is your reset button, and it's risky.

**What it fixes:** the Engineer erases every breakdown, and the Captain erases their whole route except the current dot and any mines. **What it doesn't fix:** damage stays damage, and the First Mate's gauges stay as they are.

**What it costs:** the Captain raises a fist and announces "Surfacing in sector 6!" — so the enemy now knows your sector. And while you're up, the enemy keeps playing at full speed.

**The repair ritual:**

*\[Engineers, point to the submarine drawing on your sheet.\]*

1. The Engineer traces around one of the four sections of the submarine drawing, staying inside the double white line, and writes their initials in it.
2. They pass the sheet to a teammate, who traces the next section and initials it. Then the next teammate, until all four sections are done.
3. The Engineer shows the sheet to the **enemy** Engineer, who checks no line crossed the double hull. If one did, start over.
4. Once approved, erase everything on the Engineer sheet: lines, initials, breakdowns.
5. The Engineer tells the Captain "Ready to dive!" and the Captain shouts "Dive!" — we're back in.

**While surfaced:** we can't use any systems, and our Radio Operator can't write anything down. Radio Operator, you have to **memorize** every enemy move until we dive, then catch up.

**When to surface:** when the Engineer's sheet is getting dangerous, when the route is boxing you in, or when you're sure the enemy is far away. Never when they're close. Clever trick: surface to lure them toward you through your own minefield.

## 11. Damage and the end of the game

Here's every way to take damage on one card. First Mates, you're the scorekeepers.

| What happened | Damage | Who announces it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enemy torpedo or mine lands **on** you | 2 | Our Captain: "Direct hit!" |
| Enemy torpedo or mine lands **next to** you, diagonals included | 1 | Our Captain: "Indirect hit!" |
| Our own torpedo or mine lands on or next to us | Same as above | Our Captain |
| Engineer fills a whole direction panel | 1 | Engineer shouts "Damage!"; Captain tells the enemy |
| Engineer crosses out every radiation symbol | 1 | Engineer shouts "Damage!"; Captain tells the enemy |

Always answer hits honestly and immediately. Damage to yourself is announced to the enemy too — it's part of the information war.

The moment a submarine takes its **fourth** damage, it's destroyed and the other team wins.

## 12. Recap, practice, and Dive!

**The 30-second recap:**

- Captain moves one dot and shouts the direction. First Mate charges one box, Engineer marks one breakdown, both say OK. The enemy Radio Operator draws it.
- Full gauge plus no matching breakdown means you can use the system. Shout "Stop!" first.
- Torpedo and Mine: 2 damage direct, 1 adjacent. Drone: yes/no on a sector. Sonar: one truth, one lie. Silence: a secret straight move.
- Engineer: complete circuits to repair, never fill a whole panel or all radiation.
- Surface to reset, but you give away your sector.
- Four damage and you're done.

**Practice run** *\[5 minutes; no damage counts\]*

Captains, put your X down. Both teams: "Dive!"

1. Captains, make three slow moves, one at a time. Everyone else does their job and says OK out loud.
2. Red First Mate: pretend Drone is full. Shout "Stop!" and ask Blue about a sector.
3. Blue Captain: pretend Silence is full. Shout "Stop! Silence!" and move two dots. Red Radio Operator, note it.
4. Red Captain: pretend Mine is full. Drop a Mine, make one move, then detonate it. Blue Captain answers "Clear," "Indirect hit" or "Direct hit."
5. Blue Captain: surface. Announce your sector, run the whole repair ritual, and have Red's Engineer check the sheet.
6. Everyone erases every mark. Captains pick new secret starting spots.

*\[Answer questions. Remind Captains: go slow for the first few minutes. A calm crew beats a fast, confused one.\]*

Everyone ready? Captains, on three. One, two, three — **Dive!**
